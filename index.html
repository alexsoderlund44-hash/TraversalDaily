Three dials, one score

Your Traversle score blends how much you spent, how long the journey takes, and how quickly you decided. Winning on one dial alone won't take you to the top. A cheap route that takes three days loses to a balanced one, and so does a fast route that costs a fortune.

Flight prices and times are averages of real fares for the day's cities, refreshed every morning. Land and sea legs are modelled from distance and calibrated to those fares, so a bus is always priced fairly against the plane it competes with.

When does the challenge change?

At 00:00 UTC, the same moment for everyone. The countdown on this page shows how long today's puzzle has left.

Can I play more than once?

You can replay as practice as often as you like, but only your first submission of the day counts. That keeps the global field fair.

Why is there never a direct route?

Because then there'd be nothing to plan. Every day needs at least one stop, which is where the strategy lives.

Where do the prices come from?

Flights are averaged from real one-way economy fares for the day's start, hubs and destination. Trains, buses, rideshares, ferries and cars are modelled from distance and calibrated to those fares.
